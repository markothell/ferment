A community, as a whole

Two views of one community: who is here, and what they're talking about. Both show the same example: 40 people in Hillside Tables, six circles (one still forming), ten people who haven't joined a circle yet, two open questions, and a few questions that have travelled from one circle to another. They use the circle map's colors: saffron for what was explored together, green only for what's open now, beet for one person's mark.

A circle of circles

The circle map one level up. Everyone in the community sits on the outer ring. Each circle sits inside, pulled toward the people in it, and the community's open questions hold the center, where a circle's open round would be.

Shows
Who's here, who's gathered and who isn't yet. The hollow seats are people in no circle, so a host can see who might need an invitation.
Hides
Most of the individual circle's detail. A circle shows only its size, how much it has explored, and whether something's open.
Grows
To 100 seats the outer ring still works; the dots get small and names move into the hover.

    What we're talking about

    The feed's view. Each question carries one mark for how far it has spread, and the detail of where sits one tap deeper. The rows are ordered by how many of this community's circles took the question up, which is the feed's only ranking.

    The mark
    A ripple. The dot at the middle is where the question started: filled if it started here, hollow if it arrived from another community. The inner ring fills with the share of this community's circles that took it up. The outer ring gets one tick for each other community that took it up.
    Beyond this community
    Not built yet. It's the platform-level spread from the essays, drawn now so the idea has a place. Only counts cross between communities. What people answered stays with the community that asked.
    Grows
    It doesn't matter how many circles there are: the inner ring is always a share of the whole, and the list behind a tap is as long as it needs to be.
