A community, as a whole

Three ways to draw one community. All three show the same example: 40 people in Hillside Tables, six circles (one still forming), ten people who haven't joined a circle yet, two open questions, and a few questions that have travelled from one circle to another. They use the circle map's colors: saffron for what was explored together, green only for what's open now, beet for one person's mark.

Theme
Hover or tap a circle, a person, or the center
1

A circle of circles

The circle map one level up. Everyone in the community sits on the outer ring. Each circle sits inside, pulled toward the people in it, and the community's open questions hold the center, where a circle's open round would be.

Shows
Who's here, who's gathered and who isn't yet. The hollow seats are people in no circle, so a host can see who might need an invitation.
Hides
Most of the individual circle's detail. A circle shows only its size, how much it has explored, and whether something's open.
Grows
To 100 seats the outer ring still works; the dots get small and names move into the hover.
Hover or tap a circle
2

Constellation

Each circle is drawn as a small circle map of its own: a ring of its people, with what it's explored at the middle. Wherever one person sits in two circles, a line joins their two seats. Those bridges are how the community holds together.

Shows
Overlap. You can see which circles share people and which stand alone, which is the essay's idea of connection as overlap made visible.
Hides
The community as one room. There's no outer ring, so people outside circles are only a quiet row at the bottom.
Grows
Placing circles by overlap needs a layout pass, and with 12+ circles it starts to look like a network diagram.
3

What we're talking about

The feed as a picture. Each row is a question; each column is a circle, plus the whole community. A dot marks each circle that took the question up, joined by a thread from where it started. The rows are ordered by how many circles took it up, which is the feed's only ranking.

Shows
How conversation structures spread, and adaptations as branches under the question they came from. It's the protocol-as-lever idea you can read.
Hides
People. It's about questions, not who's here, so it pairs with direction 1 or 2 more than it replaces them.
Grows
Rows are cheap. Columns are the limit: past about eight circles it needs scrolling or grouping.

Where it would live. At the top of the community page, the way the circle map sits at the top of a circle. Tapping a circle goes to that circle; tapping the center goes to the open questions.

Mixing them. 1 and 3 answer different questions, who is here and what we're talking about, and could be two views of one page. 2 is the most striking, but it's the hardest to keep calm as a community grows.
